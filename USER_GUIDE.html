What is Log Analyser?

It reads your .log files, groups repeating errors together, and shows them on a simple web page. Instead of 90 near-identical error lines, you see 1 problem that happened 90 times.

🧩Errors groupedNo more scrolling through duplicates.
🚦Clear statusSee what is New, Spiking or Known.
📍Where it happenedClass, method, file and line.
🧾Searchable logsEvery line, filtered by level, service and time.
⏱️Response timesAverage, fastest and slowest per endpoint.
📤Share itExport a CSV or a weekly digest.
🔒It runs on your own computer. Logs never leave it, nothing goes to the cloud, and it only reads your logs. It never changes them.

Get started in 3 steps

About two minutes. Nothing else to install.

DownloadGet the zip for your computer from the releases page and extract it.
Start itWindows: double-click LogAnalyser.Api.exe.
Mac/Linux: run ./run.sh in Terminal.
Open itYour browser opens at http://localhost:5171. Click Sign up (the account stays on your computer; signing up does not sign you in), then sign in.
⚠️Windows may say it "protected your PC" because the app is not code-signed. Click More info → Run anyway.

Set up your logs

You do this once, inside the app. After you sign in, the Projects page shows Get started in three steps. Tell the app what to watch (a project) and where the logs are (an environment).

Your logs must look like this

  • Files end in .log.
  • Each line starts like 2025-01-31 14:05:09,123 -- ERROR -- service-name -- message
  • Each service has its own folder. Sub-folders are fine.

Your logs look different (JSON lines, another timestamp, another layout)? See Other log formats and settings.

1. Create a project

A project is one application. Click New project, name it, and add one row per service: the folder name of its logs, without a prefix like dev-.

2. Add an environment

An environment is one place the logs live, such as dev or production. Open your project and click New environment.

Example. Your logs are in:
\\server\AKS-Dev-Logs\business-clm\dev-business-clm-analytics\ClmAnalyticsWebService
WhereFieldValue
ProjectServicebusiness-clm-analytics
EnvironmentNamedev
EnvironmentLog path\\server\AKS-Dev-Logs\business-clm
EnvironmentFolder prefixdev-
The app joins log path + prefix + service, then reads every .log file below it, including sub-folders like ClmAnalyticsWebService. You do not type the sub-folder.

After saving, wait a few minutes. The app re-checks by itself every 10 minutes by default. You can change this to 10 seconds, 15 seconds, 7 minutes or 10 minutes (see How often it checks).

Label on the environment cardMeaning
(none)Working normally
not collected yetThe first check is still running
cannot read log filesWrong path, share offline, or no permission. Hover to see why, then Edit

Read your dashboard

Click Open on an environment. You get key numbers, charts, and a table with one row per problem.

  • Search by message, service or class, and sort by any column.
  • Tick Show all to include everything. Otherwise you see only New and Spiking.
  • Click a row to see its details.
  • Export CSV saves every problem to a spreadsheet file. Weekly digest saves a one-page summary of the last 7 days (counts, top problems, busiest services, slowest endpoints) to send to your team.

Investigate a problem

Click a problem to see everything about it.

💬What happenedAn example of the error message.
📍WhereService, class, method, file and line.
🔗Trace IDClick it to see every log line of that request.
📈HistoryWhen it happened, with full stack traces.

Then decide: Suppress it if it is harmless noise, or Resolve it once you have fixed it. View logs shows all stored log lines for the problem, and Copy link gives you a link to send to a colleague.

🔒Emails, tokens, passwords, IP addresses and card numbers are replaced with [REDACTED] before they are stored or shown.

Search your logs

Open Logs in the top bar to see every stored log line, not only errors.

🔍SearchAny text, from a word to part of a stack trace. Matches are highlighted.
🎚️FilterBy level, by service, and by time range.
📖Open a lineFull message and stack trace, plus the lines around it.
📡Go liveStream new lines as they are collected.

From an open line

  • Lines around this shows what else that service logged just before and after.
  • All services shows every service around that moment.
  • Follow trace shows one request across services.
  • View problem jumps to the problem it belongs to, and Copy copies the line.

How long lines are kept

LevelKept for (default)
Error and Warn15 days
Info3 days
Debug3 days, but Debug is not stored unless you turn it on

Problem summaries and trends are kept longer (90 days). If a search finds nothing, the line may be older than its retention. You can change all of these in Settings.

💡Searches of 3 or more characters are the fastest. The time range is measured from the newest stored line, not from the clock, so a quiet system still shows its last hour.

AI analysis (optional)

Log Analyser can explain your problems in plain language using a free AI model that runs on your own computer through Ollama. Nothing goes to the cloud, and it stays off until you turn it on.

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com/download.
  2. Open a terminal and run ollama pull qwen2.5:7b (about 5 GB, one time).
  3. In Log Analyser click your name in the top bar, find AI analysis (Ollama), tick Turn on AI analysis, click Test connection, then Save.

Two extra pages then appear inside an environment:

AI analysis explains each new or spiking error with a category (Database, Timeout/Network, Authentication and so on), possible causes marked as hypotheses, and steps to investigate. Filter by service or category, and use Download report or Copy report for a ready-to-share Markdown summary.

Paste logs lets you paste any log text and get a summary, the error messages found, possible causes, investigation steps and what information is missing. Nothing is saved, and emails, IP addresses, card numbers and tokens are masked before analysis.

💡Answers are AI suggestions, so treat them as a starting point. A normal PC analyses a few problems per minute. If Ollama is not running, these pages say so and the rest of the app works as usual.

Link your code (optional)

Point a project at the folder on your computer that holds its code, and every problem page can show the code behind the error: the file the stack trace points at, the failing line with the lines around it, who last changed each line, and what changed recently.

What you get

On a problem's page, a Code card shows:

  • The file and line from the stack trace, with the failing line highlighted and the code around it.
  • Who last changed each line and when (git "blame").
  • Recent changes to this file, with a Possible cause marker on any change made in the 14 days before the problem first appeared.
  • The branch and commit it read, so you know exactly which version you are looking at.
  • Explain with code: if AI analysis is on, the local Ollama model suggests a likely cause based on the code.

Set it up

You need: git installed, and your code on your computer as git clones: either one repository, for example C:\code\business-clm-analytics, or a folder that holds several, for example C:\code\CLM with one sub-folder per repository.

  1. Open your project's edit form and find the Code card.
  2. Type the full path in Repository folder. It can be one repository, a folder that holds several repositories (every sub-folder that is a git repository is used), or several paths, one per line (or separated by ;).
  3. Click Test / Load branches. With several repositories it tells you how many it found.
  4. Pick a Branch, or leave Current branch in that folder. Leaving it means each repository uses whatever it has checked out. Picking a branch uses it in every repository that has it; the others use their current branch.
  5. Click Save code settings.

Remove clears the setting.

Prefer a terminal? Add the folder when you set up, and check it any time:

log-analyser init --repo "C:\code\business-clm-analytics" --branch main
log-analyser init --repo "C:\code\CLM"      # a folder of repositories
log-analyser doctor

doctor checks that git is installed and that the folder and branch can be read. See Command line.

🔒Log Analyser only reads your folder (with git show, blame, log and grep). It never checks out a branch, fetches, edits a file or runs your code, so your current branch and uncommitted work are untouched. Files that look like they hold secrets (.env, keys, certificates, credentials), and binary or very large files, are never shown. Code sent to the local AI model is masked first, and nothing leaves your computer.

Good to know

  • It reads the branch you chose, which may not be what is deployed, so line numbers can be off. The card says which branch and commit it used.
  • It finds the file by the end of the path in the log, then by class name, then by searching for the method name. When it is a guess, the card says so.
  • If the log has no file and line, it shows where the method is, or the start of the file.
  • With several repositories, the file is looked for in each one. The longest match of the path in the log wins, and when two repositories match equally the one whose name fits the service wins (for example business-clm-analytics prefers a repository named ...-clm-analytics). The card shows which repository the file came from.
  • File matching is tuned to the stack-trace formats Log Analyser already reads.

Command line

Prefer a terminal? Log Analyser also comes as a command line you install with npm. It runs the same app, so everything above still applies.

You need: Node 18 or newer, and a file named like log-analyser-1.4.0-win-x64.tgz from whoever shares Log Analyser with you. The platform in the name is win-x64, osx-x64, osx-arm64 or linux-x64. Pick the one that matches your computer.

npm install -g ./log-analyser-1.4.0-win-x64.tgz
⚠️Keep the ./ (or give the full path). Without it, npm thinks the file name is a GitHub user/repo and fails.

Set up and start

log-analyser init
log-analyser start

init is optional. Run on its own it only prepares your data folder and prints the next steps; it does not ask you anything. start runs the app and opens http://localhost:5171. Then:

  1. Click Sign up and create your account. This only creates the account.
  2. Sign in with it.
  3. Create your project and environment on the Projects page (Set up your logs).

Scripted setup (optional)

To do the account, project and environment from a script instead, pass flags to init: --email, --name, --password, --project, --services, --env, --path, --prefix. To also link the project's code, add --repo FOLDER and optionally --branch NAME.

log-analyser init --email you@example.com --project "Business CLM" --services business-clm-analytics --env dev --path "\\server\AKS-Dev-Logs\business-clm" --prefix dev-

Typing just log-analyser does the same as log-analyser start.

All commands

Analyse and report

analyse needs AI analysis turned on and Ollama running. On a computer without a graphics card it can take a minute or two per problem. Press Ctrl+C to stop.

log-analyser analyse --env dev
log-analyser report --env dev --out dev-report.md

If you have only one environment you can leave out --env.

Where your data lives

In a folder called .log-analyser in your home folder. To use another folder, set the LOG_ANALYSER_HOME environment variable. Log lines older than the retention window (see Logs) are dropped.

Remove it

npm uninstall -g log-analyser

Your data stays in ~/.log-analyser until you delete that folder.

Use it from an AI agent (MCP)

Let an AI assistant look at your collected logs for you. log-analyser mcp is a read-only MCP server: the assistant can ask questions, but it cannot change anything.

It reads your local data directly, so Log Analyser does not need to be running. You do need to have installed the command line and set up at least one environment (in the app, or with log-analyser init flags).

Connect Claude Code

claude mcp add log-analyser -- log-analyser mcp

Connect another assistant

Add this to the MCP settings of your assistant:

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "log-analyser": {
      "command": "log-analyser",
      "args": ["mcp"]
    }
  }
}

What the assistant can do

Try asking

  • "What are the new or spiking problems in dev?"
  • "Show me the latest errors from business-clm-analytics and what is likely causing them."
  • "Follow trace ID abc123 across all services."
  • "Which endpoints are the slowest?"
  • "What happened around that timeout, in the minute before and after?"
  • "Find the code behind problem <signature> and tell me what recently changed there."
🔒Emails, IP addresses, card numbers and tokens are masked before anything is sent to the assistant, including code from get_code_context. Results are capped at 200 lines (about 60,000 characters), so ask narrower questions if an answer looks cut off.

Other log formats and settings

Everything here is optional. Settings go in config.json, next to the program. Close Log Analyser, edit the file, and start it again. Keep the "stateDatabase": "app.db" line.

SettingUse it to
logFormatSet "json" if your logs are one JSON object per line (Serilog compact or JSON, log4net JSON layout).
logPatternDescribe another text format with a regular expression. It needs the named groups ts, level and msg; service is optional.
timestampFormatMatch the time format in your lines. If it is wrong, times show as year 0001.
eventMinLevelLowest level shown on the Logs page. Set "DEBUG" to include debug lines (uses more disk).
warnErrorEventDays, infoEventDays, debugEventDaysHow long the Logs page keeps lines per level. 0 keeps them forever.
retentionDaysHow long problem summaries and trends are kept.
maskRulesA list of expressions for ids specific to your logs, so errors that differ only by that id count as one problem.
storeEventsSet false to stop storing log lines for the Logs page.
Example. For lines like 2025-01-31T14:05:09 [ERROR] OrderService: message, add: 
"logPattern": "^(?<ts>\\d{4}-\\d{2}-\\d{2}T[\\d:]+) \\[(?<level>\\w+)\\] (?<service>\\w+): (?<msg>.*)$",
"timestampFormat": "yyyy-MM-dd'T'HH:mm:ss"

How often it checks

By default the app looks for new log lines every 10 minutes. You choose how fresh you want your data.

Click your name in the top bar and use Check logs every to pick:

10 seconds 15 seconds 7 minutes 10 minutes

It saves right away and applies within seconds.

💡Shorter means fresher data, but the app reads your log share more often.

Stop and restart

Click your name in the top bar, then Quit Log Analyser. (Mac/Linux: you can also press Ctrl+C in Terminal.) To start again, open the exe (or run.sh) as before. Your data is kept.

Common questions

Nothing shows up. Why?

Check, in order: the environment card for cannot read log files; the log path and prefix; that files end in .log; that lines match the format above (or your own format is set in config.json); and that your logs contain ERROR or WARN lines. Then wait a few minutes.

The Logs page is empty, or a line I expect is missing.

It may be older than its retention (Error and Warn 15 days, Info 3 days), or it is a Debug line and Debug is not stored by default.

Performance is empty.

It needs ASP.NET Request finished ... - 200 - ... 12.3ms lines in your logs.

"Too many failed sign-in attempts".

After 5 wrong passwords for one email, that email is locked for 15 minutes. Wait, then try again.

How fresh is the data?

Every 10 minutes by default. You can choose 10 seconds, 15 seconds, 7 minutes or 10 minutes on your account page. It is not real time.

Does it change my logs?

No. It only reads them.

Where is my data stored?

Next to the program, in app.db and the environments/ folder. Keep them when you update.

How do I update?

Download the new zip and replace only the program file (LogAnalyser.Api.exe, or LogAnalyser.Api and run.sh). Leave config.json, app.db and environments/ alone.
